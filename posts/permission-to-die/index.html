What really is “permission”?

So, let’s start from the investigation of the phenomena that we observed it time to time: Ad hominem, an act of attacking person instead of their argument. Common word in academic, especially in philosophy. Fun question is: why is that a common word? If you are in academic world, you should be familiar with the concept of “attacking the argument, not the person”. Yes, in academic world. But, in real life? That is something that we see commonly. “Their face is the same as their thought” or “You should reduce your weight before talking about health”. Like, this is a normal discourse we see in the general non-academic discussion.

We can go on and discuss more on other fallacies. The point is, what gives them the right to say that? Well, free speech yes but I am not talking about that kind of right. I think I might need to go back to when I was in high school to illustrate this idea. So, a simple question, what makes people look “bully-able”? Say, you see obese person, do you have the thought of talking to them “fat” when they did something you don’t like? I am talking about that kind of “right”, or more so, “permission” to say that.

Or, what kind of speech or the way they talk makes you want to mock them for it? Because maybe it is not because of who they are, but how they view the world. Easiest way to see this is through the lens of political fraction. Suppose you support party A, which might be conservative. Then, what about party B, which can be liberal? I don’t think I need to explain further on how that will end up because I assume everyone reading this is familiar with said concept. If not, go read the news. Look at the comment on the news about protest. Or not even that, suppose we are talking in academic term, how to not get mocked by “influencer” or “gatekeeper” in that field just by explaining what you understand?

That essence of “what leads people into bullying” is what I meant by “permission”. What allows them to morally or logically justified their action that is, from the third-person perspective, is wrong? What traits resulting in suffering of the one who has that?

On suicide

Now, when people thinking about suicide, it is almost a taboo to not investigate on it from pessimism perspective. It is, as someone has said, horror of philosophy. It is common that people would say “there is too much to live for” as a motivation speech. Or “your life matters” and other similar phrases. Those mean nothing to the person who is suffering. It is like saying to the Sisyphus, “keep rolling, your rolling matters”. But it is also easy to say “just die your life does not matter” when you don’t even considering about the consequences or morality of death. Surprisingly, dying costs money. It causes suffering, specifically, to someone who wants you to stay alive.

That begs a lot of questions: “Is wanting someone to stay alive a selfish need to keep you from suffering yourself?” or “does medical staff actually care about you or just doesn’t want to get investigated?” and much more. The answer for those questions are not exactly easy to investigate. Not like we can think for others, but still, doesn’t prevent us from asking their motives. But if someone really care and want to commit suicide, that also raise another interesting question: “how to turn that motive, specifically against you, to be negative”?

Say, pick utilitarianism as a moral philosophy. Essentially, we want to maximize the welfare or happiness of everyone. If we are living in suffering, one could say, not living is better from hedonic calculus perspective. But, problem is, each individual of us are not independent. That is, if we were to commit suicide, you are reducing your suffering and you might get happy, but others will feel sad. How can we turn the suffering from others into their happiness? That is where the “permission to die” coming into the play.

Permission to die

Suppose we want to commit suicide, so we plan ahead by making everyone suffer from our existence. Not because we actually harm them physically or being an asshole, but being people who could, in theory, “bully-able”. That essence that causes people to be asshole towards you is what we want to justify our death. Once everyone is happy with our death, then we did a moral thing of maximizing the happiness. We manipulate them into thinking that our death is deserved so that no one will feel sad or have moral objection on our suicide. Well, at least from Bentham’s theory (hence, why hedonic calculus), and not necessary Mills’ theory on utilitarianism. As Mills might argue that this is no difference in comparison with making everyone drunk and call that “happiness”.

But that doesn’t stop us from asking crucial question that, while utilitarianism allows us to be pessimistic to that point (and also not), does that justify the morality of people who committed suicide? This is just one of the major moral philosophy paradigm and yet here we are having the hard time argue about it. If everyone are happy to not see you again, would leaving morally justifiable? If we manufacturing that said situation, would that be morally justifiable?

There is another concept that usually understate which is antinatalism. This is generally overshadowed by the right of abortion. If abortion is justifiable then why you are the one who invented that suffering in the first place? Why would we just not, you know, talk about antinatalism instead of abortion? Oh yes, 9 months are long enough to change the decision, but we argue that it doesn’t matter. Then, why does ending our lives are not the same? I changed my mind on living so I should have my right to, you know, end it. Why it has to be someone else who take the moral decision of you being alive or death? Like, that fetus doesn’t ask to be born in the first place, yet when they are now born, we say actually, no. Playing the role of god deciding whether they are alive or not because you don’t think it through in the first place. Yet, we, as a society, put the blame into the one who committed suicide as “not thinking it through”. Because abortion is not a taboo but suicide is a taboo, is that it?

If autonomy is sufficient to justify refusing continued existence before or after birth, then autonomy should carry significant weight in a competent person’s decision to end their own life. Yet, here we are in the society where “you should not die” but killing a fetus is OK. We are living in a society where we don’t have autonomy in our lives. We need permission to not exist to be morally acceptable. We need other people to allow us to die. Why can’t we just, simply, say good bye and be done? Why we have to entangled our lives with others to the point that dying is not an option? We say, your life is yours but put moral responsibility and burden into the one who suffered and want it to end. Clearly, we own our own life. But also, we embrace intervention for someone who just want to end it. We say that is not a right thing to do. Then, what is a right thing to do? Who dictates that?

Dying costs something. Clearly, one part is money to dispose your body. But also the burden on people who has to do it. Then, from this perspective, is burden of disposing or taking care of the mess something you want to avoid? Then, is that said burden the motivation for intervention? Or the burden of not having them “contribute to the society”? Or anything that justified your “keep going” mindset? Isn’t it the same as “you look bully-able”? Both are the same somehow. That is, one has special moral duty to intervene the person’s wish somehow. One is to not get bullied but well, “bully-able”. another one is “I want to end it” but well, “you have so much to live for”.

Can we exploit this mechanism so that, if we want to die, we can die? Would it still be moral if we exploit this chain? What is worth so much to live for and why that is morally superior to, end it all? What justifies the intervention and how can we make sure that doesn’t exists? How can we make ourselves into “deserved to die”? Say, would you kill Hitler because he did holocaust with his justification of something common at that time: antisemitism? What makes you think killing Hitler is OK? Do I have to be like Hitler to die? I am not saying Hitler is good, but at which point we say a person deserved to die? One can argue that Hitler radicalize the antisemitism and push it further. Not saying that is justifiable morally, but also if we think about it, was Hitler also a victim himself? It doesn’t erase the fact that he killed (through proxy and what not) a lot of people. It doesn’t erase the fact that he caused suffering to the entire world. But can a bad guy also a victim of the society where moral autonomy is just, not there? How much autonomy is required before another person may legitimately override your decision? Hitler had ability to stop, fix, and atone at anytime. He did not. He was a victim but also did not try to make it right. But interestingly, dying is one time thing. You die and it is ended, you can’t stop being dying. How can we be so gullible that we can just die? Is dying justifiable if we can’t fix it?

We are not on the binary decision on whether suicide is good or bad. But we are at the situation that, why can’t we just make a decision to ourselves through our autonomy and ability to think of it? How to minimize the consequence of dying so that it is justifiable to die? These are why “permission to die” is needed. What really is the moral basis of “suicide” and “suicide intervention”?
